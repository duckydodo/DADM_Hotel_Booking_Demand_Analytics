Methodological Note on Selection Bias:
 Restricting the pricing analysis to completed stays introduces survivorship/selection bias.
 Canceled reservations may have had higher prices or greater price elasticity (e.g. guests
 cancel after finding a cheaper alternative). Thus, findings strictly reflect conditional
 observational associations of REALIZED demand rather than unconditional market quotes.

KEY BUSINESS TAKEAWAYS & MANAGERIAL IMPLICATIONS (OBSERVATIONAL ASSOCIATIONS):
1. Seasonal Pricing Divergence:
   - August achieves the highest rate premium (+74.17 EUR/$, p < 2.2e-16) relative to January baseline.
   - Low season months (November, January, February) exhibit substantial baseline rate suppression.
2. Room Type Tiering:
   - Premium suites command substantial premiums: Room G (+60.16 EUR/$) and Room H (+63.84 EUR/$)
     over baseline standard Room A (p < 2.2e-16).
3. Party Size Associations:
   - Each additional adult is associated with approximately +13.33 EUR/$ higher ADR (p < 2.2e-16).
   - Each additional child is associated with approximately +13.98 EUR/$ higher ADR (p < 2.2e-16).
4. Channel & Segment Concessions:
   - Online Travel Agencies (OTAs) are associated with higher unit ADR (+9.47 EUR/$) than Direct bookings,
     while wholesale Groups (-9.89 EUR/$) and Corporate (-11.46 EUR/$) trade at negotiated discounts.
5. Model Architecture Benchmark:
   - CART achieved superior out-of-sample prediction (Test R2: 66.53% vs MLR: 61.16%)
     because it inherently captures non-linear seasonal interactions (Resort x August).
   - MLR provides indispensable, interpretable marginal pricing coefficients for revenue management.
   - Caution: These relationships reflect conditional observational associations ceteris paribus,
     not causal interventions.
