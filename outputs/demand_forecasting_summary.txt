--- STEP 1: ALLOCATION OF COMPLETED STAYS TO CALENDAR ROOM-NIGHTS ---
Weekly Time Series Construction Details:
 - Calendar Coverage: 2015-07-06 to 2017-08-27 (exactly 112 full 7-day weeks)
 - Target Variable: 'weekly_occupied_nights' (sum of daily occupied rooms across each 7-day week)
 - Zero Value Audit: No zero-demand weeks exist (Resort Min: 350 | City Min: 153)
   --> MAPE is mathematically defined and well-behaved across all evaluation periods.

Limitations of Using Historical PMS Extracts to Reconstruct Occupancy:
 1. Left-Censoring: Guests who checked in prior to July 1, 2015 but stayed into July are missing from
    the raw extract. Starting our full series on July 6 (Week 2) mitigates initial ramp-up artifacts.
 2. Right-Censoring: Arrivals terminate on August 31, 2017. Truncating at August 27 avoids end-of-sample decay.
 3. Physical Capacity Unrecorded: The PMS extract records transactions, not physical room caps.

--- STEP 2: COMPARATIVE TIME SERIES PROPERTIES (WHY MODEL SEPARATELY) ---
Resort Hotel Weekly Occupancy: Mean = 1055.4 | SD = 264.6 | Min = 350 | Max = 1277
City Hotel Weekly Occupancy  : Mean = 1189.2 | SD = 382.2 | Min = 153 | Max = 1563

Operational Rationale for Separate Modeling:
 - Resort Hotel demand displays severe seasonal volatility (soaring to ~1,277 in summer, dropping to ~350 in winter)
   governed by holiday schedules and weather.
 - City Hotel demand is more stable throughout the year (~1,189 average), driven by corporate business
   and urban weekend leisure, with shorter stay durations.
 - Pooling them would obscure their fundamentally different volatility structures and staffing requirements.

--- STEP 3: CHRONOLOGICAL TIME SERIES SPLIT ---
Training Period: Weeks 1 - 78 (2015-07-06 to 2017-01-01 | 78 complete weeks)
Testing Period : Weeks 79 - 112 (2017-01-02 to 2017-08-27 | 34 complete weeks)

Note on Seasonal Decomposition:
 Classical seasonal decomposition (STL or decompose) requires at least two complete seasonal cycles
 (2 x 52 = 104 weeks of training data). Because the training set covers 78 weeks (1.5 years),
 seasonal decomposition cannot be reliably estimated and is responsibly not forced.

================================================================================
MANAGERIAL IMPLICATIONS FOR HOTEL STAFFING & CAPACITY PLANNING
================================================================================
1. Practical Utility of Forecast Errors:
   - Simple Exponential Smoothing (SES) achieved the lowest test RMSE for both properties:
     * Resort Hotel Test RMSE = 93.7 room-nights (MAPE = 6.32%)
     * City Hotel Test RMSE = 113.5 room-nights (MAPE = 6.16%)
   - Scale Conversion Note: Dividing weekly RMSE by 7 yields an average scale conversion of
     ~13.4 (Resort) to ~16.2 (City) rooms/day. This provides a rough order-of-magnitude
     illustration for intuitive scaling, NOT a true measure of daily operational forecast
     uncertainty. Day-to-day fluctuations, weekend arrival surges, and intra-week variance
     are smoothed out by weekly aggregation. Day-level operational uncertainty requires daily modeling.
2. Housekeeping & Rostering Allocation:
   - Housekeeping shifts depend directly on daily occupied rooms rather than arrival counts.
   - Proposed Heuristic Policy: Core permanent staffing could be set to cover ~90% of expected
     weekly demand (or baseline minus 1 SD), utilizing flexible on-call agency staff to absorb surges.
     This is a proposed operational heuristic policy requiring local cost-benefit validation
     (comparing idle wage costs against agency overtime premiums), rather than an empirically solved optimum.
3. Food & Beverage (F&B) and Perishable Procurement:
   - Buffet breakfast prep and perishable culinary ordering (BB and HB meals) can be adjusted
     one week in advance based on SES projections, directly curbing food waste.
4. Why Seasonal Naive Lag-52 Underperformed:
   - SNaive produced the highest test MAPE (10.9% for Resort, 12.2% for City) because it fails to
     account for secular growth between 2016 and 2017. Both hotels experienced structural growth,
     causing a purely historical prior-year lag to systematically under-forecast demand.
5. Short Historical Span Limitation:
   - The entire dataset spans only 26 months. Because the training set covers just 1.5 years (78 weeks),
     we observe only ONE previous winter and ONE previous summer. Long-term cyclical or macroeconomic
     claims cannot be validated without multi-year longitudinal observation.
